How to Raise Your FICO Credit Score Fast: The 5 Official Scoring Factors Explained

M
Mariana Castro, Financial Analyst
September 29, 2026 9 min read
Share:

Your three-digit FICO Score (ranging from 300 to 850) determines the interest rate you pay on mortgages, auto loans, and credit cards—costing or saving tens of thousands of dollars over a lifetime. Here is how the official algorithm works.

1. Payment History (35% of Your FICO Score)

On-time monthly payments represent the single largest factor in your score. A single payment reported 30+ days late can drop a strong score by 60 to 100 points. Enable autopay for at least the minimum due on every account as a safety net.

2. Credit Utilization Ratio (30% of Your Score)

Utilization measures your reported statement balances divided by your total credit limits. Keeping utilization below 10% across all cards—by paying down your balance two days before the monthly statement closing date—can boost your score within a single billing cycle.

"Paying down your credit card balance before the statement closing date—not just the due date—is the fastest legal way to lower reported utilization."

3. Length of Credit History (15%), New Credit (10%), and Credit Mix (10%)

Avoid closing your oldest no-annual-fee credit card, as doing so reduces your average account age and shrinks your total available credit limit.

Disputing Inaccurate Negative Items for Free Under the FCRA

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), you are entitled to free weekly credit reports from Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion at AnnualCreditReport.com. Bureaus must investigate and remove unverifiable errors within 30 days of a formal dispute.
