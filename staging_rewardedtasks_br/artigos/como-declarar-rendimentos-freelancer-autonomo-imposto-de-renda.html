Saver's Credit, EITC & Child Tax Credit: Top IRS Tax Credits That Directly Reduce What You Owe

M
Mariana Castro, Financial Analyst
November 02, 2026 8 min read
Share:

While a tax deduction only lowers your taxable income by your marginal percentage bracket, a tax credit reduces your IRS tax bill dollar-for-dollar. Millions of eligible US taxpayers overlook credits worth hundreds or thousands of dollars each spring.

1. The Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)

Designed for low-to-moderate-income working individuals and families, the EITC is a refundable tax credit—meaning even if you owe $0 in federal income tax, the IRS sends the remaining credit amount to you as a direct refund.

2. The Retirement Saver's Credit (IRS Form 8880)

Eligible moderate-income taxpayers who contribute to a 401(k), 403(b), Traditional IRA, or Roth IRA can claim a tax credit worth 10%, 20%, or 50% of their first $2,000 ($4,000 married filing jointly) in retirement contributions—stacking on top of traditional IRA deductions.

"Contributing to an IRA or 401(k) when you qualify for the IRS Saver's Credit gives you an immediate tax-time bonus on top of long-term compound growth."

3. American Opportunity & Lifetime Learning Education Credits

Paying college tuition or upskilling through accredited courses can qualify for up to $2,500 per student via the American Opportunity Tax Credit (AOTC) or up to $2,000 per return via the Lifetime Learning Credit.

Using IRS Free File and VITA for $0 Tax Preparation

Taxpayers within IRS AGI thresholds can file federal returns completely free through the official IRS Free File program or Direct File, avoiding $150+ commercial software upsells.
