High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSA) vs. Treasury Bills (T-Bills): Where to Park Your Cash in 2026

M
Mariana Castro, Financial Analyst
October 03, 2026 9 min read
Share:

Leaving cash in a standard big-bank checking account earning 0.01% APY means losing purchasing power to inflation every month. Comparing High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs) against short-term US Treasury Bills helps you earn maximum safe yield.

FDIC Insurance vs. Full Faith and Credit of the US Government

Online High-Yield Savings Accounts are protected by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category. US Treasury Bills (4-week to 52-week maturities) are backed directly by the US Treasury.

The State & Local Income Tax Advantage of T-Bills

While interest earned on an HYSA or bank CD is subject to both federal and state income taxes, interest earned on US Treasury Bills is 100% exempt from state and local income taxes—a major boost for residents in high-tax states like California or New York.

"Keep one month of living expenses in an instant-access FDIC High-Yield Savings Account, and ladder the rest of your emergency fund in state-tax-exempt T-Bills."

Liquidity and Same-Day Access Considerations

An HYSA allows instant transfers to your checking account 24/7 with zero market price fluctuation. T-Bills lock your cash until maturity (unless sold on the secondary market via a brokerage account like Fidelity, Vanguard, or Schwab).

How to Build a Simple 4-Week T-Bill Ladder

By splitting your reserve into four equal buckets maturing one week apart, one-fourth of your cash becomes liquid every single week while capturing Treasury yields.
